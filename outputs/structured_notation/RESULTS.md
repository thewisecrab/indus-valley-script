## Errors found in the source papers (checked against mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus @ ad2f1e2)

- **Reading direction.** Kriger & Hunt's positional entropy table (3.13, 4.44, 4.45, 3.77, 3.98) matches our values in the JSON's visual left-to-right order, which is the reverse of the reading order (the JSON records the sealing; Indus is read right to left). In reading order, the lowest entropy is at the final position (3.13), where the jar sign stands.
- **Sign labels in Diener's Appendix C.** P324 is given as the "Unicorn" in P1, but P324 is the jar sign (Mahadevan 342). P385 is given as "Sign 342", but P385 is the diamond-shaped sign (Mahadevan 267), which is text-initial in reading order.
- **Damage marker.** Diener's "draft" record M-141 ("[P000, P122, P385] without P385") is P000 P122 P000 in the data. P000 is the corpus's marker for "a section of significant damage or lost material", not a sign. M-19, M-110 and M-175 also begin with P000. We report results with and without damaged texts.

## Deviations from the pre-registration

1. **B, titles.** Wikipedia titles are unique by construction (one article per title), so their 100% is not counted in the B decision. The decision rests on Latin all-genre inscriptions, DCS Sanskrit spans and running-text windows. With the titles counted, the result would be the same.
2. **Length matching.** Some comparison corpora have too few texts of a given length. `matched()` then takes the nearest available length (mean lengths sampled: Sanskrit titles 4.17, Barn stars 3.94, Pictish 4.13, Vinča 2.19, against a target of 5.6). These corpora are reported but are not used for any decision.
3. **A2 permutations.** The held-out real-vs-shuffle comparison used 200 shuffles per seed (minimum p = 1/201), not 2,000. A2 used samples of 1,000 texts (the full I1 for the 179-seal scale). With Kriger & Hunt's 179 seals, there are 2 seeds with enough held-out texts, not 5.
4. **A1 comparison set.** Kudurrus, Barn stars, Pictish and Vinča have too few 5-sign texts for A1, so they are absent from A1.
5. **Bug fixed before the full run.** A quick development run used de-duplicated Indus texts for B, which makes uniqueness 100% by construction. The full run uses the texts as recorded.
6. **CDLI proto-cuneiform benchmark (optional).** Skipped for time and because of licensing uncertainty. Not run.

## Plain-language summary

Indus texts do have strong positional habits. Some signs nearly always come first, and the jar sign nearly always comes last. Most sequences are unique. These facts are real and replicate. But each of them is also found in short texts written in known languages, especially owner's stamps, name-like texts and phrases, so none of them separates a "non-linguistic cargo tag" from short written language. The two specific predictions that would have been distinctive both fail: numerals do not cluster in a fourth field, and the animal on the seal does not fix the size of the numeral. Many texts are also too long to fit five fields. These tests do not show that the script records language; they show that these arguments do not show it does not.
